BEFORE TESTING

Bench checklist

  1. Confirm the matrix, analyte, reporting basis, and method edition.
  2. Log sample ID, depth or tissue stage, storage, and collection time.
  3. Use your controlled SOP for preservation, preparation, hazards, and waste handling.
  4. Document calibration, blanks, controls, acceptance limits, and deviations.
  5. Verify the method meets the client, accreditation, and regulatory scope.

Laboratory safety protocol

Use this checklist with your laboratory’s approved procedures, safety data sheets (SDS), and local emergency plan.

Before starting

Review the procedure and SDS. Identify exposure routes, incompatible reagents, waste streams, and emergency contacts. Check that eyewash and safety shower access is clear. Verify that required ventilation and protective equipment are ready.

During the work

Use splash protection and gloves compatible with the reagent. Use a working chemical fume hood for operations that require vapor or aerosol control. Label preparations with their identity, concentration, date, and relevant hazards. Secure gas cylinders and use the regulator specified for the gas.

Spill or exposure

Stop work and alert nearby staff. For a hazardous spill or gas release, leave the area and call the site emergency contact. For chemical splash to eyes or skin, start flushing immediately at the eyewash or safety shower and seek medical assistance under the emergency plan.

After the work

Place chemical waste in compatible, labeled containers and segregate incompatible streams. Follow the site waste procedure. Decontaminate the bench and equipment, wash hands, and record incidents and corrective actions.

Scope & sources

This catalog summarizes selected published procedures. The FAO field screening panel draws on FAO’s 2020 Soil Testing Methods guide. Its entries include original quick protocol summaries and printed page references. It is a method-selection and training reference. Use the source edition and your lab’s controlled SOP for bench instructions, acceptance criteria, and safety. Regulatory acceptance and accreditation depend on the method, matrix, and jurisdiction.
